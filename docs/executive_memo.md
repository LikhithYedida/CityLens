# Executive Memo: Where Opportunity Is Growing in America

October 2026. Housing data through September 2026. Prepared by Likhith Yedida.

## Bottom line

The places where a typical household can still afford a home and find a growing job market are concentrated in Texas, the Southeast and mid-size Midwestern cities, led by Huntsville (AL), Austin (TX), Sherman-Denison (TX), Raleigh-Cary (NC) and Sioux Falls (SD). The coasts remain the most stretched. Home-price momentum has moved north: the pandemic boomtowns of Florida and Texas are cooling, while the Midwest and Northeast are rising. Over the next 12 months, rent in a typical metro is expected to rise about 3%. Among large metros, the slowest growth is expected in Austin, Dallas, San Antonio and Orlando.

## What the data shows

1. **Only 62 of 379 metros are both affordable and growing.** Among large metros, that group is Austin, Raleigh-Cary, Dallas, San Antonio, Houston, Oklahoma City, Indianapolis and Columbus. In San Francisco, Providence-Warwick and Boston, a mortgage on a typical home takes 38% to 52% of a typical household's income.
2. **Price growth has changed direction.** 19 of 24 metros flagged to outgrow the national market next year are in the Midwest or Northeast. 55 of 56 cooling boomtowns are in the South and West. Before 2022, metros gaining residents saw faster price growth. Since 2022 they have seen slower growth.
3. **Growth spreads about 75 miles.** When metros within 75 miles grow faster, a metro tends to grow 0.45 points faster the following year (per standard deviation), and the pattern held before, during and after the pandemic. 26 affordable metros have neighbors pulling ahead, including Eau Claire (WI), Watertown-Fort Drum (NY), Fargo (ND), Bloomington (IL) and Oshkosh-Neenah (WI).
4. **The forecasts beat simple rules.** Rent forecasts missed by 3.1 points of 12-month growth on average over 2020-2025, against 3.5 for the best simple rule, and 1.8 against 2.7 since 2022. Their 80% ranges contained 94% of outcomes since 2022.
5. **Zoning reform helped Minneapolis, but less than reported.** Minneapolis rents are 16% below a comparison built from 180 cities, but its own suburbs, which the reform did not cover, fell about 8% as well. The reform explains at most about 8 to 10 points of lower rent.

## What to do with it

**Households.** Among large metros, the strongest combination of cost and job growth is in Austin, Raleigh-Cary, Dallas and San Antonio. Rents in Austin, Dallas and San Antonio are forecast to stay roughly flat over the next year (-0.2% to +0.2%), which gives renters room to negotiate.

**Builders and lenders.** Demand is moving toward Rising Cities such as Springfield (IL), Abilene (TX) and Binghamton (NY), and toward Next Ring Out metros such as Eau Claire (WI), Watertown-Fort Drum (NY) and Fargo (ND). In the cooling boomtowns, plan for flat prices and slower lease-up.

**City and regional leaders.** Upzoning is worth doing, but Minneapolis suggests it moves rents by single digits, not by a third. Because growth spreads about 75 miles, housing supply works best when planned across a region rather than one city at a time.

## What could change this view

- National shocks drive most one-year price changes. The 2020-21 surge and the 2022 rate shock broke every forecast tested here, including this one.
- Migration data runs two years behind and job data six months behind.
- The Rising City and ripple signals predict well, but they do not prove cause and effect.

## How the work was done

Seven public sources (Zillow, Census ACS, Census building permits, BLS, IRS, the Federal Reserve and Census geography) were joined into a tested panel of 935 metros by month since 2015. Each finding comes from a separate, documented analysis: a weighted index with 1,000-weighting sensitivity tests, panel regressions with walk-forward backtests, a synthetic control with placebo tests, gradient-boosted forecasts with conformal ranges, and spatial statistics. Every judgment call is in the decisions log.
